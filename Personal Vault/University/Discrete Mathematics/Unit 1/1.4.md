###### Base definitions
**tautology**, if it is always true, regardless of the truth values assigned to its variables
**contradiction**, if it is always false, regardless of the truth values assigned to its variables.
Two compound propositions are **logically equivalent** if they have the same truth value regardless of the truth values of their individual propositions

###### Proving logical equivalence
**To prove:** show with a truth table that both columns are the same
**To disprove:** find the two corresponding values in the column with different truth values

###### Methods to prove logical equivalence
**Method 1:** Construct the whole truth table and find one row that is / .
**Method 2:** Find one set of truth values that cause the compound proposition to evaluate to false / true

###### Example: Prove that p → q, ¬p ∨ q, and ¬q → ¬p are logically equivalent

| F   | T   | T    | T   | F   | T      | T        |
| F   | F   | T    | T   | T   | T      | T        |
###### De Morgan's laws - the negation of "*^*" and "*v*" 
(a) ~(p *^* q) = ~p *v* ~q
(b) ~(p *v* q) = ~p *^* ~q

###### Example 3. What is the negation of “The student is a math major or has taken MATH405? ”
the student is (a math major) or (has taken math 405)
~((math major) *v* (taken math405)) 
(~math major) *^* (~taken math405)
the student is not a math major and has not taken math405

###### Example 4. What is the negation of p → q? What is the negation of “If ab = 0, then a = 0”?
negation of p->q, what is the negation of "if ab=0 (p), than a=0 (q)"
ab =/0 *v* a=0
~(ab=/0 *v* a=0)
ab=0 *^* a=/0
